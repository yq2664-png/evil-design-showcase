Selected projects

Three recent collaborations, each built around a single clear idea and a system that carries it.

Onda Identity

Onda Coffee — A warmer daily ritual

Brand identityPackaging2024

Onda came to us as a neighborhood roaster ready to grow without losing its hand-made feel. We built a flexible identity around a hand-drawn sun mark, a warm clay palette, and a typographic voice that moves from quiet shelf presence to loud street posters.

The result: a system that scales from a single bag to a 40-foot market wall while still feeling like it was made by people who know your order.

Field Campaign

Field Notes — A campaign for slower looking

Art directionCampaign2023

For a stationery brand moving into a crowded lifestyle category, we resisted the urge to add noise. The campaign paired large-format photography of actual working desks with a restrained, almost scientific overlay system — inviting people to slow down and look closely.

The work ran across print, out-of-home, and a digital series, and became the foundation for the brand’s next two seasons.

Morrow Editorial

Morrow Studio — An editorial system with room to breathe

Editorial designVisual system2024

Morrow publishes long-form interviews with architects and makers. The existing design was beautiful but brittle. We rethought it as a modular system: a strong grid, a small set of type styles, and a color logic that lets each issue feel distinct without losing the family resemblance.

The new system cut production time in half and gave the editors room to make playful choices again.

About Mina

A decade of making brands feel more like themselves — and less like everyone else.

I’m an independent art director working across identity, campaign, and editorial. My practice sits between strategy and craft: I care about the big idea, and I care just as much about the kerning on the last line.

I’ve led design at Studio Halcyon and North & Co., and now collaborate directly with founders, in-house teams, and other studios. I’m based in Brooklyn and work with clients worldwide.

“Good art direction doesn’t decorate a message. It decides what the message is allowed to be.”
