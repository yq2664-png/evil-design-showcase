01 / Selected work

Three stories. Three different worlds.
Brand strategy · Visual identity2025

Morrow

A quieter kind of skincare. An earthy, expressive identity that brings everyday rituals back to nature.

The challenge

Make a botanical skincare newcomer feel grounded and distinctive in a category full of clinical minimalism.

The approach

A soft, organic wordmark meets field-note typography, botanical linework, and a palette pulled from the garden. Packaging puts the daily ritual ahead of the ingredient list.

My role

Positioning, art direction, visual identity, and packaging system. An independent concept, not a commercial launch.

Art direction · Campaign2025

Offbeat Festival

For the beautifully out of step. A loud, flexible visual world for a festival where music, art, and happy accidents collide.

The challenge

Bring an eclectic program together without sanding down its personality.

The approach

Oversized type, unexpected angles, and an asterisk that refuses to sit still. A modular identity built to remix across posters, tickets, and stages.

My role

Creative concept, art direction, campaign identity, and print applications. An independent festival concept.

Editorial · Art direction2024

Still Here

Making room for the overlooked. An independent journal finding extraordinary stories in ordinary objects, spaces, and moments.

The challenge

Create a print-first publication that makes slowing down feel like an invitation, not an instruction.

The approach

Generous margins, tactile colors, and thoughtful changes of pace. Quiet still-life compositions sit alongside intimate stories, giving small details the space to become the main event.

My role

Editorial concept, publication design, and still-life art direction. A self-initiated publication study.

02 / The person behind the pixels

Curiosity is the starting point.

Good work.
Good people.
No autopilot.

I’m Mina, an independent art director based in Brooklyn. I work with people who care deeply about what they make—and want the world to care, too.

My practice moves between strategy, identity, and image-making. I like a sharp idea, an unexpected color, and the moment when something finally feels right. From the first big question to the smallest printed detail, I’m equal parts thinking partner and hands-on maker.

Away from the screen: collecting odd little objects, judging books by their covers, and taking the long way home.
