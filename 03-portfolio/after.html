Morrow
Identity / Packaging
01

Morrow

Morrow is a small-batch skincare label built around the idea of slow morning rituals. The brand needed an identity that could live on shelf, online and in editorial contexts without losing its quiet confidence.

I developed a hand-drawn sun mark, a warm neutral palette and a flexible packaging system spanning glass bottles, cartons and refill pouches. The work extended into a street poster series and a set of social templates that the in-house team now runs independently. The result is a cohesive visual language that feels handmade but scales cleanly across production.

Offbeat
Campaign / Poster
02

Offbeat Festival

Offbeat is an independent music festival in the Catskills focused on experimental and genre-fluid acts. For its fifth edition, the organisers wanted a campaign that felt raw and immediate — something that could stand out on a crowded wall of posters.

I built a typographic system using a single condensed grotesque, layered with photocopied textures and a strict two-colour palette. The campaign rolled out across posters, wristbands, a pocket schedule and a looping title sequence for the festival's social channels. Every asset was designed to be printed or posted at short notice by a volunteer team.

Still Here
Editorial / Digital
03

Still Here

Still Here is a digital archive and editorial platform documenting independent art spaces that have survived gentrification. The founders needed a visual identity that could hold long-form interviews, photo essays and a growing map of locations.

I designed a modular grid system, a restrained type palette and a set of editorial templates that let the small team publish without a designer. The identity uses a single accent colour and a consistent image treatment to tie together contributions from dozens of photographers and writers. It launched alongside a print reader distributed at independent bookshops.

About

Mina Chen is an independent art director working across identity, editorial and campaign design. She splits her time between Brooklyn and Tokyo, collaborating with cultural institutions, independent labels and small teams who care about craft.

Her practice is grounded in typography, systems thinking and a belief that good design should feel inevitable rather than decorative. She works directly with founders and in-house teams, from first sketch to final production file.
